01 / FOUNDATIONS

What is a Typeface?

“

A typeface is the name given to the specific letterform design system of an alphabet.

  • 01
    Universal Abstract Design System: Type is one of humanity’s most universal visual systems, communicating meaning beyond raw spoken language.
  • 02
    Cultural & Historical Reflection: The physical shapes of letters reflect the architecture, tools, philosophy, and political eras in which they were created.
  • 03
    Technological & Societal Forces: Technical advancements in printing presses, paper quality, metal founding, and cultural reading habits dictate type evolution.
Logical Grouping Rule: Typefaces are grouped by function and the period in history in which they were created.

04 / AXIS OF EMPHASIS

The Four Types of Stress

Stress refers to the direction where the thickest part of a letter appears. It reveals where calligraphic pressure was applied.

Why Stress Matters: Affects readability • Dictates personality • Guides font choice for tone & purpose.
DYNAMIC STRESS SIMULATOR Axis: ~ -20° Diagonal
Diagonal Stress (Oblique)
Simulates the natural 45° angle of the human hand holding a broad-nib reed or quill. The thinnest parts appear at the upper-right and lower-left, while maximum weight settles along the diagonal.
05 / TERMINAL ARCHITECTURE

Serifs & Brackets

A serif is a small decorative line attached to the ends of letter strokes. A bracket is the curved or angled fillet that joins the serif to the main stem.

BRACKETED SERIF

Garamond • Baskerville

Curved fillet gently smooths the 90° junction between the stem and horizontal serif. Imparts flow and structural softness.

UNBRACKETED SERIF

Bodoni • Didot

Abrupt, razor-sharp perpendicular join without any transitional curve. Produces dramatic, mechanical severity.

06 / HISTORIC ERA 01

Old Style Typefaces

1617 Garamond (France)
  • 01
    Low Stroke Contrast: Very subtle, modest difference between thick and thin strokes across all letters.
  • 02
    Heavy Brackets: Generous curved fillets supporting the serifs, providing structural strength in early metal casting.
  • 03
    Extreme Diagonal Stress: The axis of weight follows the natural 45° forward angle of the calligrapher’s hand.
  • 04
    Human vs. Machined: Overall aesthetic feels organic, warm, and distinctly handcrafted rather than geometrically manufactured.

07 / HISTORIC ERA 02

Transitional Typefaces

1757 Baskerville (England)
  • 01
    The Historic Bridge: Bridges the evolutionary gap between warm Old Style and austere Modern typefaces.
  • 02
    Refined Serifs & Increased Contrast: Sharper, more calculated serifs with noticeably higher stroke contrast than Garamond.
  • 03
    Shift to Vertical Stress: Weight distribution rotates upright toward a near-vertical axis, moving away from the diagonal slant.
  • 04
    Retains Bracketing: Still incorporates heavy bracketing, preserving humanist warmth despite a more machined precision.

08 / HISTORIC ERA 03

Modern Typefaces

1788 Bodoni (Italy) • Giambattista Bodoni
  • 01
    Not Modern by Our Timetable: Created in the late 18th century, representing a revolutionary break from calligraphic tradition.
  • 02
    Extreme Stroke Contrast: Spectacular disparity between massive solid stems and razor-thin hairline strokes.
  • 03
    Unbracketed Hairline Serifs: Serifs attach at crisp 90° right angles with little or no bracketing.
  • 04
    Strict Vertical Architecture: No noticeable stress angle; perfectly plumb vertical emphasis creating formal grandeur.

09 / HISTORIC ERA 04

Slab Serif Typefaces

1894 Century Expanded (US) • Egyptian / Square Serif
  • 01
    Heavy Blocky Serifs: Characterized by prominent, rectangular square serifs with consistent stroke weight.
  • 02
    Advertising & Industrial Clarity: Engineered during the Industrial Revolution for legibility on crudely printed posters, flyers, and newspapers.
  • 03
    Early vs. Clarendons: Early designs had zero bracketing and zero contrast. Later, Clarendons reintroduced subtle brackets and refined stroke contrast.
  • 04
    Modern Usage: Blends both Clarendon sophistication and bold non-bracketed industrial slab geometry.

10 / HISTORIC ERA 05

Sans Serif Typefaces

1957 Helvetica & Univers • Bauhaus & Art Deco Influence
  • 01
    Simple Machined Shapes: Clean, unadorned terminals and uniform stroke appearance without extra flourishes.
  • 02
    Bauhaus & Art Deco Catalyst: Around since early 19th century, but popularized 100 years later by modern design movements.
  • 03
    The Vertical Reading Tendency: Because of the lack of serifs, sans serifs tend to read vertically rather than the traditional horizontal flow.
  • 04
    The Leading Solution: Adding more leading (space between lines) restores effortless horizontal reading continuity.

11 / SYSTEMATIC VARIATIONS (PART 1)

Type Styles: Weight

Type style usually refers to the variety of weights or widths of the letters in a specific typeface. Many names are given to the same weight or width of a letterform.

1

Medium (Standard Baseline)

Often referred to as regular, book, or normal. It represents the most basic or standard weight of a typeface, engineered for sustained text readability.

2

Light (Thin Variant)

Light may sometimes be called thin. It is the lighter, thinner version of the medium typeface, often used for elegant display headings.

3

Bold (Heavier Spectrum)

A general term for a heavier variation. Depending on heaviness, specialized terms are used: demi-bold, semi-bold, heavy, extrabold, ultra, or black.

11 / SYSTEMATIC VARIATIONS (PART 2)

Width: Condensed & Extended

Width dictates letterform proportions, altering horizontal and vertical eye flow across words and text blocks.

4

Condensed (Compressed / Narrow)

Also referred to as compressed or narrow. The narrow version of a typeface that gives letterforms the distinct appearance of a vertical line direction. Perfect for saving horizontal space in headlines.

↕ Appearance of a Vertical Line Direction
5

Extended (Expanded)

Extended or expanded version of a typeface is wider than the standard medium typeface. Because of the emphasis on width, it creates a prominent horizontal line direction across the line.
